\section*{1.\ Introduction}
This project proposes a corpus study of Mandopop between 2000 and 2020, with a music-theoretical emphasis on \emph{form} (harmony, sectional design, phrase periodicity, repetition strategies) and production techniques, with the core goal to operationalize and test claims about increasing \emph{homogeneity} (or decreasing diversity) in measurable musical features over time.

The project synthesizes: (i) a literature-grounded account of music-theoretical corpus approaches to popular-music form and stylistic change \citep{serra2012,mauch2015,leveillegauvin2015,duinker2020,shea2020diss,shea2022sampling,shea2024diversity}; (ii) a scalable, largely automated audio-corpus workflow paired with \emph{off-the-shelf} MIR procedures as measurement instruments for form and sound; and (iii) An LLM-agent-driven interpretive layer asks how measured musical shifts can be read through debates about platformization, digital sovereignty, and gender politics---including whether changes correlate with constraint/oppression or new affordances for women in Mandopop’s star/producer ecology.

\section*{2.\ Research questions}
% The project answers research questions (RQs), designed to stay anchored in music-analytical evidence while enabling cultural interpretation:

The historical frame begins in 2000 (shortly before China’s WTO accession) and ends at 2020 to avoid confounding the analysis with post-pandemic shocks. I assert 2009, 2012, and 2018 as \emph{candidate} inflection points motivated by changes in China’s platform/media ecology and governance narratives.\footnote{These dates are chosen as plausible phase boundaries in the political economy of Mandopop circulation: circa 2000, Mandopop’s industrial ecology and repertory increasingly localize, with a declining reliance on cover adaptations and imported stylistic templates (e.g., J-pop/Cantopop cover pipelines and externally anchored rock idioms); circa 2009, the post-crisis environment coincides with intensified metricization of attention (traffic/clicks/plays) as a coordination mechanism for promotion and production; circa 2012, a conspicuous cooling of the cultural field following a high-visibility period of political spectacle coincides with a reorientation toward large-scale consolidation of domestic digital cultural infrastructure and licensing regimes, plausibly reshaping incentives; and circa 2018, the core infrastructure appears comparatively mature, alongside heightened content governance and compliance pressures that may increase risk-aversion and standardization.} %These hypotheses will be tested using change-point methods and compare them to data-driven breakpoints.
The measured results will be used to assess whether, and in what dimensions, Mandopop’s mainstream stylistic space becomes more homogeneous (or more diverse), and to determine how any shifts align with the proposed historical phases. Research questions below are designed to stay anchored in music-analytical evidence while enabling cultural interpretation:

\textbf{RQ1 (Longitudinal change):} How do distributional properties of Mandopop \emph{formal organization} change from 2000--2020 (e.g., motivic ressemblance, phrase periodicity)? How do these relate to established pop/rock form concepts \citep{covach2005form,nobile2020} and to function-oriented formal thinking \citep{caplin1998}?

\textbf{RQ2 (Homogeneity/diversity):} Do within-year diversity measures and between-year distances show increasing homogeneity in form, melody, and timbre/production? If so, which musical facets drive the trend (e.g., narrowing of sectional prototypes vs.\ narrowing of timbral palettes)?

\textbf{RQ3 (Change points):} Are there statistically significant change points around the hypothesized years 2009, 2012, and 2018? If not, what breakpoints emerge from the musical data, and how stable are they under resampling and alternative corpus constructions? 

\textbf{RQ4 (Gendered \& media patterns):} Do formal/timbral trajectories differ by artist gender (and, where possible, by role: vocalist vs.\ producer/composer credits)? Can measured differences be plausibly connected to gendered expectations in Chinese popular music industries, without reducing interpretation to a single causal story? How can these findings be interpreted alongside scholarship on China’s platformization of music circulation and governance discourses around digital/cyber sovereignty?

\section*{3.\ Methodology}

% state-of-the-art but \emph{off-the-shelf} music information retrieval (MIR)


% The study applies state-of-the-art but \emph{off-the-shelf} music information retrieval (MIR) techniques as analytical instruments, conducted on a stratified corpus of Mandarin-language popular recordings released between 2000 and 2020, sampled evenly across years and major circulation channels to enable longitudinal comparison. Corpus construction, demographic encoding, and representational limitations are documented transparently and evaluated through sensitivity analyses.


\textbf{Corpus construction.}
A large-scale corpus of Mandarin-language popular recordings released between 2000--2020 will be assembled via automated collection across major circulation channels.\footnote{Because automated collection may yield uneven coverage across years and channels, longitudinal estimates could be calibrated using weighting and/or stratified resampling.} Track records and associated metadata will be scraped programmatically, including platform/channel provenance where available, and each item will be assigned a stable identifier. Duplicate or near-duplicate versions (e.g., re-releases, remasters, live recordings) will be deduplicated or explicitly versioned, and corpus decisions will be logged for auditability and replication.


\textbf{Data Pre-processing.} 
Audio files are standardized for comparability, then processed by state-of-the-art but \emph{off-the-shelf} music information retrieval (MIR) techniques \citep{mcfee2014structure,mcfee2015librosa,nieto2020structure,declercq2016measuring,burgoyne2011chord}.
For example, predominant melody could be estimated using contour-based approaches \citep{salamon2012melody}; beat and downbeat grids could be estimated using widely adopted tracking pipelines \citep{bock2016madmom}; metric interpretations could be encoded probabilistically where analytic ambiguity is plausible \citep{declercq2016measuring}; formal organization could be modeled through structural segmentation based on self-similarity and repetition, using spectral clustering approaches \citep{mcfee2014structure,nieto2020structure}. 




\textbf{Statistical Inference}
Year could be modeled both continuously (trend estimation) and discontinuously (change-point detection). Candidate breakpoints (2009, 2012, 2018) could be tested against data-driven alternatives using change-point methods and bootstrap uncertainty estimates. Gender-stratified analyses evaluate whether trajectories differ across artist gender and, where possible, across credited roles. 

% The inferential goal is modest but precise: identify which musical dimensions show robust narrowing/widening and whether temporal discontinuities are stable under resampling, alternative corpus constructions, and feature substitutions.

\textbf{Constrained semantic induction.}
The interpretive layer will use an LLM-agent workflow to produce an auditable \emph{semantic map} linking quantitative outputs (e.g., trend estimates, prototype shifts, change-point results) to a bounded set of interpretive constructs relevant to platformization and gender politics.\footnote{The model is not applied to raw audio; it is prompted only with structured, human-readable analytical summaries and controlled metadata (e.g., year/phase/gender/role), and it returns \emph{candidate} relation statements that explicitly point to the specific empirical results they depend on and specify what counterevidence would weaken them. Methodologically, this functions as a standardized, reproducible interpretive-coding step that complements statistical inference (which estimates \emph{whether} patterns exist) by systematically organizing \emph{how} those patterns can be related to theory-facing concepts.} The agent’s knowledge input is constrained by a curated concept vocabulary/codebook grounded in relevant scholarship such as on popular-music analysis and \textit{Formenlehre} concepts \citep{covach2005form,nobile2020,caplin1998,Schmalfeldt2009Process,VandeMoortele2015Formal}, and all prompts/inputs/outputs plus expert adjudication decisions are logged for traceability.

The LLM is \emph{not} applied to raw audio; it only receives structured, human-readable analytical summaries (tables of effects, descriptions of structural prototypes, and robustness outcomes) plus controlled metadata (year/phase/gender coding). The agent proposes candidate semantic linkages as \emph{auditable hypotheses}, each required to (i) cite the specific quantitative evidence it depends on (e.g., which feature and which trend/change-point result) and (ii) state what additional tests or counterevidence would weaken the linkage. A critic step (human and/or second-agent) filters for overreach and eliminates causal claims not supported by the inference layer. Accepted linkages are recorded in a ``claim ledger'' (inputs, prompts, outputs, human decisions), making the semantic layer reproducible and reviewable rather than impressionistic. knowledge input could for example draw on popular-music form scholarship \citep{covach2005form,nobile2020} and on functional concepts adapted from the \textit{Formenlehre} tradition \citep{caplin1998,Schmalfeldt2009Process,VandeMoortele2015Formal}.

\section*{4.\ Contributions}


% \textbf{Contribution:} 



The project contributes (1) a reproducible Mandopop corpus with documented sampling decisions and bias auditing, enabling a large-scale audio-based map of stylistic change; (2) an empirically grounded intervention within in academic discourse that demystifies the persistent popular myth about the decline of ``quality''; (3) a model for integrating MIR-derived evidence with interpretation about platform/media dynamics and gender politics.



The study will assemble a stratified corpus of Mandarin-language popular recordings released between 2000 and 2020 with approximately even sampling by year and documented inclusion/exclusion rules; each track will be assigned a persistent identifier and linked metadata (release year, circulation channel/provenance where recoverable, artist identity, credited roles such as vocalist/composer/producer where available, and artist gender coding with documented uncertainty). Audio will be standardized prior to analysis, and mid-level musical representations will be derived via candidate MIR pipelines: predominant melody could be estimated using contour-based approaches \citep{salamon2012melody} to compute pitch range, interval distributions, contour archetypes, and repetition statistics; beat and downbeat grids could be estimated using widely adopted tracking pipelines (e.g., implemented in \textit{madmom}) \citep{bock2016madmom}; metric interpretations could be encoded probabilistically where analytic ambiguity is plausible \citep{declercq2016measuring}. Formal organization could be modeled through structural segmentation based on self-similarity and repetition, using spectral clustering approaches \citep{mcfee2014structure,nieto2020structure}; 



Harmonic organization could be represented using chroma-based descriptors and coarse loop typologies, supplemented where reliable by automated chord estimates benchmarked against expert-annotated resources \citep{burgoyne2011chord}. Production and timbral characteristics could be modeled using spectral features, loudness proxies, and embedding-space representations (e.g., computed with \textit{librosa}) \citep{mcfee2015librosa}, informed by perceptual approaches to timbre dimensions \citep{McAdams1995Perceptual}. Formal interpretation will draw selectively on popular-music form scholarship \citep{covach2005form,nobile2020} and on functional concepts adapted from the \textit{Formenlehre} tradition \citep{caplin1998,Schmalfeldt2009Process,VandeMoortele2015Formal}. Homogeneity will be operationalized using within-year dispersion measures and between-year distributional distances across formal, melodic, harmonic, and timbral features, including prototype concentration/entropy for structural patterns; change-point analyses will compare hypothesized break years (2009, 2012, 2018) with data-driven alternatives, with uncertainty assessed via resampling; gender- and role-stratified analyses will evaluate whether trajectories differ by artist gender and credited roles where available. An LLM-based agent workflow will generate and organize candidate semantic relationships linking quantitative outputs (feature trends, prototype shifts, change points, robustness results) to interpretive constructs related to platformization and gender politics, with candidate relationships recorded as a claim ledger (inputs, prompts, outputs, and expert decisions) for traceability. Approximately 10\% of the corpus will be manually audited to assess extraction and segmentation reliability, and longitudinal claims will be accompanied by robustness checks under alternative feature sets, parameterizations, and corpus constructions.
